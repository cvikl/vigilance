# Actions and owners live in a hash-exempt protocol block; the workbench may order the clinician's review queue

*2026-09-16, after the finalised track constraints (docs/rubric_fit.md).*

**Actions block.** `protocol.yaml` gains a top-level `actions:` section: an `owner` per criterion and, per reason code, a `class` (`system` = the patient is stalled by the service; `legitimate` = the patient is legitimately waiting, e.g. `patient_declined`, `contraindication_documented`, `dna`) and an `action` (the specific next step). `canonical_hash` excludes this block, as it already excludes `locked` and `hash`. A verdict is a function of criteria, events and reason codes only; who owns the fix and whether a cause is legitimate are operational metadata that a trust may change between audit rounds without re-adjudicating. Consequence: protocol hash `83174236e120` and the 6,220 archived verdicts stay valid; every alert and every reported action carries an owner and an action that a human wrote into the protocol, not one the model invented. Alternative rejected: fields inside `criteria` (changes the hash → full S5 re-run for a metadata edit).

**Review queue.** The workbench orders cases for clinician review and prints the reason for the order (abstain, unverified evidence, model call disagrees with the computed status, largest excess over target, largest effect on the interval). This prioritises the clinician's *checking*, not the patient's care: no row is a recommendation, risk score or alert about an individual patient, and the estimator treats every reviewed row the same whatever its rank. ADR 0002 stands — nothing patient-level leaves the boundary; the "why this handoff, why now" prioritisation that leaves the boundary is at criterion / organisation / week level only.
